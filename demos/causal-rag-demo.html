Ask the model. Then cross-examine it.

Causal-RAG gives a language model causally adjusted evidence about real loans, then edits the applicant and the evidence to see whether the model reacts the way the causal estimates say it should. It ends with an auditable decision and a causal graph. Everything below is measured on real data: 589,099 Lending Club loans, real policy text and a real randomised trial.

Is the causal estimator any good on real loans?

Checked against randomisation. In the Hillstrom e-mail experiment customers were assigned at random, so the experimental difference is a trustworthy answer.

Every model recovers the average effect, but only the neural models rank customers by benefit: Qini 31 to 32 against 9.3 for the T-learner, and calibration slopes near 1 against 0.12.

Does handing that evidence to an LLM help?

Take one ingredient away. Each row is a paired comparison on the same 600 applicants (85 defaults). Rows that cross zero are not distinguishable.

Listing the applicable risk flags (0.668) is statistically indistinguishable from the full causal pipeline (0.678). The matched precedent and the CALF numbers each add nothing measurable on top of the flags.

The cliff test

Real risk changes smoothly across a cutoff like 25% DTI. Does the model's answer? For each clause the model is asked just below and just above the cutoff, and over an equally wide interval far from it.

The audit rules, in your hands

1 unit = the “near the threshold” band. Positive means the model leans to decline.

What the audit did on 600 real applicants (smooth-prompt audit, v1.2).

Flags raised:

Default rate rises from approved to referred to declined, but the groups are small. Approving the referred cases as well would lift the approved-group default rate from 11.5% to 12.5%: referral removes about one point of risk. Most referrals come from thin matched evidence, not from detected inconsistency.

Mistakes we found in our own work

What this shows

What worked
  • CALF's effect heterogeneity is well calibrated for FICO, loan-to-income and DTI, and checked against a real randomised trial.
  • Placebo, random-common-cause and subset refuters pass for every clause.
  • The audit tools caught our own errors: the flag-listing effect, an easy sign test, a winner's curse and a flawed cliff metric.
What did not
  • No LLM variant is significantly better than the plain LLM; tabular models are at least as good.
  • Layering the covariates added nothing over a flat network, as in the CALF paper's insurance result.
  • Describing conditions as cutoffs makes the model's answer step-like; the label prompt jumps 86 times more across a cutoff than away from it.

Limitations

Clause effects are observational (E-values 1.8 to 2.1; utilization is fragile at 1.06). Lending Club contains funded loans only. The four cutoffs are analyst-defined: the retrieved policy text states none of them. The language-model comparison uses 600 applicants with 85 defaults, one 4-bit 7B model and single runs, so differences below about 0.05 AUROC are within noise. Nothing here is a lending decision system.
