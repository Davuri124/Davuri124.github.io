Who gets the human reviews?

Human reviewers are scarce: a realistic limit is "at most K reviews per 1,000 queries". This project builds a multi-agent pipeline that really runs, then asks what a learned orchestrator can add on top of a tuned, non-learned baseline when that hard cap is enforced. No synthetic data: every action the orchestrator could take was executed once on real questions and cached.

    Spend the budget: how much does each method earn?

    Net reward = answer F1 minus compute cost (re-retrieval 0.02, bigger model 0.05; a human review counts as F1 = 1). Real results on 2,000 held-out test queries.

    Bars start at 0.40. "Oracle" is an unachievable hindsight ceiling, not a baseline.

    Where the reward comes from

    Cheap automatic upgrades (wide re-retrieval) earn far more than anything a policy adds on top. Early comparisons against human-only baselines were misleading for exactly this reason.

    What is not finished

    Confirmation on fresh data is pending. The design choices were guided by test-set numbers, so the gains above may be optimistic. A pre-registered test on 2,000 further, disjoint questions is set up (policies, risk models and thresholds frozen; Holm-corrected one-sided tests at budgets 0.02, 0.05 and 0.10; the evaluation refuses to run if any frozen file changed). The fresh cache is built; the evaluation itself has not been completed, so nothing here claims replication.

    Limitations

    One dataset (HotpotQA) and one model family (Qwen 3B and 7B). The human reviewer is an oracle that always supplies the gold answer. Compute costs are assumptions, and the ranking of methods changes with them. The stacked gains are small (about 0.5 to 1.7 points of net reward) and individually borderline. Everything is offline replay of real runs, not live serving.
